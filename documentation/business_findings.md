# Business Findings: Four Quarters of 2024

Scope: 2,303,556 scheduled US domestic flights by BTS reporting carriers in four months of 2024, one from each quarter (February, June, September, December), covering all four seasons.
Each month is a snapshot of its season, so month-to-month differences reflect season together with the specific weather and events of that month.

## Headline numbers

| Measure | Four quarters of 2024 |
|---|---:|
| Flights scheduled | 2,303,556 |
| Non-cancelled flights departing 15+ minutes late | 19.6% |
| Completed flights arriving on time (under 15 minutes late) | about 80.5% |
| Cancelled | 0.80% |
| Diverted | about 0.23% |

## 1. Which airlines have the highest delay rates?

On-time arrival ranged from 73.5% (Frontier) to 86.6% (Republic).

| Airline | Flights | On-time arrival | Avg departure delay | Cancellation rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Delta | 328,111 | 83.9% | 8.6 min | 0.21% |
| United | 248,081 | 81.9% | 9.9 min | 0.72% |
| Southwest | 457,861 | 80.7% | 10.9 min | 0.42% |
| American | 318,897 | 76.6% | 17.5 min | 1.06% |

- Delta leads the four largest carriers on every measure. American is weakest, with an average departure delay about double Delta's.
- Across four seasons the gap between American and Delta is 7.3 points of on-time arrival. By month it was 7.5 points in February, 12.3 in June, 6.4 in September and 3.0 in December, so the single hardest month overstated the difference. American ranked 11th or lower of 15 carriers in every month.
- Southwest has the highest share of late departures among the large carriers (22.3%), but its average arrival delay (4.0 min) is far below American's (11.3 min). Its delays are frequent and short.
- Regional carriers Republic (86.6%) and Endeavor (85.1%) lead the table, and Hawaiian is third (84.9%). Republic arrives 3 minutes early on average, which points to generous schedule padding.
- JetBlue (74.5%) and Frontier (73.5%) have the lowest on-time arrival. Frontier (1.61%), Endeavor (1.44%), JetBlue (1.36%) and Republic (1.35%) have the highest cancellation rates.

Carrier rankings reflect network and hub exposure as well as operations. Hawaiian benefits from island routes with little congestion, and carriers with hubs in congested or storm-prone regions rank lower partly for reasons outside their control.

## 2. Which airports experience the most disruption?

Among airports with at least 500 departures, the highest departure delay rates were Aspen (33.1%), Aguadilla (28.0%), Fort Lauderdale (26.2%), Sanford (26.2%), Miami (25.8%) and Dallas/Fort Worth (25.2%).

- **Aspen** stands apart: one flight in three departs late and 6.9% of its flights are cancelled, the highest cancellation rate in the table. It is a mountain airport with limited operating margins.
- **DFW** has the highest delay rate of the large hubs over the four months: 102,381 departures at 25.2% late with a 16.1 minute average delay. Atlanta, at a similar size (111,443 departures), runs at 18.1% and 9.0 minutes. DFW's position is driven by its two worst seasons: it ranked first among the 15 busiest airports in June (36.2%) and December (26.6%), third in September (19.6%) and sixth in February (17.1%).
- Among the 15 busiest airports, the highest delay rates are DFW (25.2%), Charlotte (23.8%), San Francisco (22.7%), Las Vegas (22.6%), Orlando (22.4%) and Denver (22.0%). The lowest are LaGuardia (17.1%), Detroit (17.4%), Los Angeles (17.9%), Atlanta (18.1%) and Washington National (18.2%).
- LaGuardia has the lowest delay rate of the large airports but the highest cancellation rate among them (1.58%).
- Lowest delay rates overall: Hilo (8.2%), St. George (9.5%), Great Falls (9.9%), and the Hawaiian islands Kahului, Lihue and Kona (10.6% to 10.9%).
- Taxi-out times are longest at Chicago O'Hare (23.5 min) and LaGuardia (23.3 min).

## 3. Which routes are most delay-prone?

Among routes with at least 100 flights, arrival delay rates peaked at Dallas/Fort Worth to Albany (50.9%), San Francisco to Aspen (47.8%), Miami to Portland (47.3%) and Aspen to San Francisco (46.9%).

- Aspen appears in four of the 15 worst routes (to San Francisco, Atlanta and Houston, and from San Francisco), which matches its airport-level result.
- DFW appears in four (Albany, Syracuse, Hartford, and Key West inbound).
- Each route has between 104 and 321 flights over the four months, so the ranking is indicative. Routes at the top are consistent with the airport findings.

## 4. What are the primary causes of delay?

Share of delay minutes on flights arriving 15+ minutes late:

| Cause | Share |
|---|---:|
| Late-arriving aircraft | 39.8% |
| Carrier | 35.4% |
| National Aviation System | 18.5% |
| Weather | 6.1% |
| Security | 0.2% |

The overall mix is almost identical to June alone (41.5%, 34.4%, 17.8%, 6.1%), so it is a stable feature of the system and not a one-month result. It differs sharply by airline:

- Late-aircraft delay is over half of delay minutes at Frontier (52.8%), Southwest (51.9%) and PSA (51.4%), and close to half at American (47.0%).
- Carrier-controlled causes dominate at Hawaiian (63.1%), SkyWest (51.1%) and Delta (47.3%).
- Spirit has an unusually high NAS share (39.8%).
- Direct weather share is highest at SkyWest (15.8%), Allegiant (10.9%) and Envoy (10.2%).

## 5. Does weather significantly influence punctuality?

Directly, by BTS definitions, a little. Overall, it matters more than that figure suggests.

- BTS "weather" accounts for 6.1% of delay minutes. That category covers extreme weather that directly delays a flight; weather-driven air traffic control restrictions are counted under NAS, and knock-on delays fall under late aircraft.
- Weather is the dominant reason for cancellation: 62.2% of the 18,528 cancellations (11,529), ahead of carrier (21.7%) and NAS (16.1%). By month, weather accounted for 63% of cancellations in February, 50% in June, 74% in September and 75% in December. Direct weather delay stayed between 4.8% of delay minutes (September) and 7.5% (December).

## 6. Are flights more likely to be delayed in particular seasons?

Yes, clearly. Punctuality moves sharply with the season:

| Month | Flights | On-time arrival | Avg departure delay | Late departures | Cancelled | Diverted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| February | 519,221 | 84.1% | 7.4 min | 15.5% | 0.58% | 0.18% |
| June | 611,132 | 75.1% | 16.9 min | 25.3% | 1.29% | 0.32% |
| September | 582,622 | 84.5% | 6.6 min | 15.1% | 0.60% | 0.15% |
| December | 590,581 | 78.9% | 13.5 min | 21.6% | 0.70% | 0.27% |

- **June is the hardest month** by every measure, and **September and February are the easiest**. The gap between June and September is 9.4 points of on-time arrival and 10.2 points of late departures; average departure delay is 2.5 times higher in June (16.9 against 6.6 minutes), and the cancellation rate is about double.
- **December sits in between**: 21.6% late departures, with on-time arrival 5.6 points below September.
- Each month is a snapshot of its season, so these differences combine seasonal pattern with the weather and events of that month.

**The daily build-up is the same in every season; only the level changes.** In every month the late-departure rate climbs from early morning to a peak around 8pm, and the 8pm rate is 3.4 to 4.8 times the 5am rate.

| Month | 5am departures late | 8pm departures late |
|---|---:|---:|
| February | 5.8% | 22.8% |
| June | 8.4% | 40.3% |
| September | 5.6% | 24.1% |
| December | 9.4% | 32.0% |

| Delta | 88.7 (3) | 80.2 (5) | 87.1 (4) | 80.4 (6) |
| United | 84.0 (6) | 75.8 (7) | 86.8 (5) | 81.2 (3) |

- **Consistent top performers:** Republic, Endeavor and Delta stayed in the top six in every month.
- **Consistently behind:** American, Frontier and JetBlue stayed in the bottom five in every month.
- **Biggest seasonal swings:** Frontier (16.4 points between February and June) and Envoy (16.0 points, from 87.7% to 71.7%). SkyWest (7.1 points), Alaska (8.3) and Delta (8.6) were steadiest.
- **Hawaiian** was first in June and December and second in September, but only 12th in February (81.0%).
- **PSA** declined month after month, from 86.3% in February to 72.6% in December (rank 5 to rank 14), a 13.8 point drop.
- **United** and **Southwest** improved toward winter: United from 6th to 3rd, Southwest from 10th in June to 4th in December.
- **American's gap to Delta** narrowed from 12.3 points in June to 3.0 in December.

## 7. Which days and departure times see the most delays?

Time of day is the strongest pattern in the data.

| Scheduled departure | Departed 15+ min late |
|---|---:|
| Before 9am | 9.3% |
| 9am to 3pm | 18.0% |
| 3pm to 8pm | 26.4% |
| 8pm onward | 28.7% |

The delay rate is about 7.4% for 5am and 6am departures and peaks at 30.5% for the 8pm hour, roughly four times higher. Delays accumulate through the day as late aircraft feed later flights. The June-only figure (40.3% at 8pm) was higher because June is the highest-delay month; the same build-up appears in every season (see Question 6).

Day of week shows a clear pattern across the four seasons:

| Day | Late departures |
|---|---:|
| Sunday | 22.3% |
| Friday | 21.1% |
| Saturday | 20.6% |
| Thursday | 19.7% |
| Monday | 18.9% |
| Wednesday | 17.4% |
| Tuesday | 16.3% |

Sunday, Friday and Saturday are the worst days, Tuesday and Wednesday the best, a spread of about 6 points. Flight distance matters less: short flights under 500 miles are late 18.1% of the time, against about 20.3% for medium and long flights.

## 8. How does late-arriving aircraft affect subsequent flights?

For the same aircraft's next departure from the airport it just landed at (2.2 million flight pairs):

| Inbound flight arrived | Next flights | Next departure 15+ min late | Avg next departure delay |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Early | 1,382,123 | 8.9% | 1.9 min |
| On time (0 to 14 min late) | 376,474 | 17.1% | 7.7 min |
| 15 to 59 min late | 283,275 | 50.2% | 22.1 min |
| 60+ min late | 133,128 | 62.5% | 68.5 min |

A late inbound aircraft raises the chance of a late next departure from under 9% to over 50%. This is an association: part of it reflects conditions shared by both flights, such as weather affecting both ends of a trip, not only the knock-on effect.

## 9. Can historical data predict future delays?

Yes, and the predictions hold up on a season the model never saw. Trained on February and June, validated on September and tested on December (586,430 flights, 21.6% late), gradient boosting reached ROC-AUC 0.764 and PR-AUC 0.516, against a no-skill PR-AUC of 0.216. That is about 2.4 times better than chance at identifying late departures. Predictions are made 60 minutes before scheduled departure using only information available at that time.

| Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Logistic regression | 0.734 | 0.457 |
| Random forest | 0.754 | 0.492 |
| Gradient boosting | 0.764 | 0.516 |

Threshold-based precision, recall and baseline comparisons are in `model_results.md`.

**Where the predictive power comes from** (gradient boosting, 60-minute lead):

| Feature set | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Scheduled departure hour only | 0.615 | 0.278 |
| + airline, origin, destination, distance, weekday | 0.639 | 0.312 |
| + prior 7-day airport and airline delay rates | 0.644 | 0.315 |
| + previous flight of the same aircraft | 0.742 | 0.462 |
| + same-day airport conditions (last 3 hours) | 0.678 | 0.399 |
| + previous flight and same-day conditions | 0.760 | 0.510 |
| All features | 0.764 | 0.516 |

- Departure hour alone gives a meaningful signal (0.615), consistent with the delay build-up through the day.
- The previous flight of the same aircraft is the biggest single gain, lifting ROC-AUC from 0.639 to 0.742. In operational terms, the model is mostly reading late-arriving aircraft, the largest delay cause.
- Same-day airport conditions (the delay rate and average delay of flights that had already departed from the same airport in the previous 3 hours) add a further 0.018 ROC-AUC and 0.048 PR-AUC on top of the previous-flight signal, and help on their own (0.639 to 0.678).
- The prior 7-day delay rates add a small gain (0.005 on their own, 0.004 in the full model).

**Effect of prediction lead time** (all features):

| Prediction made | Previous-flight status known | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 60 min before departure | 57.8% | 0.764 | 0.516 |
| 120 min before | 35.7% | 0.703 | 0.423 |
| 180 min before | 27.2% | 0.682 | 0.394 |

Accuracy falls as the lead time grows because the inbound aircraft is often still airborne or has not yet departed. Even at 180 minutes the model stays ahead of schedule-only features (0.682 against 0.639).

## 10. What operational improvements could reduce delays?

These are hypotheses suggested by the data, to be tested.

1. **Add schedule buffer in the afternoon and evening.** Late departures rise from 9% before 9am to 29% after 8pm, and late-arriving aircraft is the largest delay cause. Buffers in aircraft rotations or ground time later in the day address knock-on delay directly.
2. **Prioritise rotation resilience at carriers where late-aircraft delay exceeds half of delay minutes** (Frontier, Southwest, PSA).
3. **Strengthen storm-season recovery at DFW.** It has the highest delay rate of the large hubs over the four months (25.2% against Atlanta's 18.1%) and ranked first among the busiest 15 in June and December, so summer and winter disruption recovery there has the biggest payoff.
4. **Treat carrier-controlled delay as the target** where it dominates (Hawaiian, SkyWest, Delta), since those minutes are within the airline's control.
5. **Plan mountain airports like Aspen for their operating margins.** At 33% late and 6.9% cancelled, extra buffer and recovery capacity matter most there.
6. **Look at Friday to Sunday capacity.** Sunday runs about 6 points worse than Tuesday, which suggests peak-demand days need more slack.
7. **Plan buffers and staffing by hub and season.** Summer peaks hit CLT, DEN, DFW, LAS, MCO and ORD; December peaks hit ATL, DCA and LGA; San Francisco peaks in February. Seasonal schedules and recovery resources can follow those patterns.
8. **Use the early-warning model operationally.** With a 60-minute lead it identifies late departures 2.4 times better than chance and can trigger gate, crew and passenger recovery actions earlier.

## Scope and method notes

- Four months of 2024, one per quarter; US reporting carriers and domestic flights.
- Weather is measured through BTS cause codes; external weather observations are not joined.
- Findings describe associations in this data, not causation.
- Small airports and routes rest on small samples, and the minimum-volume rules in the queries guard against the noisiest cases.
- The model is evaluated chronologically on whole months, so the test month is a season it has not seen.
